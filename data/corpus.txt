The transformer reads a sequence of tokens and predicts the next token from context.
In each block, root mean square normalization stabilizes the hidden state before attention.
Rotary position embeddings encode relative distance directly into query and key vectors.
Grouped query attention shares key and value heads across multiple query heads to save memory.
SwiGLU combines a smooth gated activation with linear projections for strong representation power.

In the quiet lab, small neural models train step by step with clean gradients and steady loss.
First, the tokenizer maps text into discrete integer tokens from a compact byte vocabulary.
Next, mini-batches of sliding windows feed into the causal self-attention layers.
A causal mask ensures that every token attends only to itself and earlier positions in the sequence.
Then, cross-entropy loss measures how closely the predicted probabilities match the target token.

During backpropagation, reverse-mode automatic differentiation traces every tensor operation.
Gradient norm clipping prevents sudden spikes when multiple layers stack deep residual paths.
The cosine learning rate schedule warms up gently and decays smoothly toward the minimum rate.
AdamW updates parameters with momentum, adaptive variance scaling, and decoupled weight decay.

After training, the model saves its weights to a SafeTensors checkpoint on disk.
SafeTensors stores each tensor with exact dtype metadata and contiguous binary buffers.
During inference, autoregressive decoding generates one new token at a time from a prompt.
Temperature scaling and top-k filtering balance coherence and diversity in the generated text.
The transformer learns grammar, structure, and rhythm from the patterns inside the corpus.
Attention weights focus on relevant earlier words to complete each phrase with clarity.
In the quiet lab, every experiment is reproducible, verifiable, and grounded in measured loss.
